04 / Why this matters

A tax system with a long memory.

Proposition 13 generally limits a property’s annual assessed-value growth to an inflation factor of no more than 2%, with reassessment commonly triggered by a change in ownership or new construction. The basic property-tax rate is 1% of assessed value, with additional voter-approved debt and other charges.

That structure can give otherwise similar properties very different tax bases. A long-held property can carry a much lower assessed value than a recently purchased one, even when their estimated market values are close.

We believe those differences deserve public scrutiny. The purpose of this project is to make the evidence accessible so residents, researchers and policymakers can examine who benefits from the current system and what a fairer one could look like.

The records do not establish when every owner acquired a property, household income, or why a specific assessment differs. This study examines a system, not the intentions of individual homeowners.

05 / Methods & limitations

Reproducible by design

The numbers need their context.

01. What is covered

Loading coverage…

This is an address-matched observational dataset, not a random sample or a certified assessment roll. Duplicate units, multi-parcel homes, valuation errors and imperfect address matching can affect comparisons.

02. What each value means

Estimated value: an automated home-value estimate from a third-party property website, observed on the recorded capture date. It is not an appraisal, a sale price or an official tax assessment.

Assessed value: the assessment reported by that same website. The assessment year is shown when available; otherwise it is explicitly unknown.

Reported annual tax: the most recent positive tax-history amount available as of capture. It is not independently verified with the county and does not establish that the bill was actually paid.

03. How comparisons are calculated

Assessment gap: estimated value minus assessed value. Assessment share is assessed value divided by estimated value.

Annual benchmark gap: 1% of estimated value minus reported annual tax. The reported-tax-to-estimate ratio divides the reported tax amount by estimated value.

The 1% reference uses only the basic rate. It excludes local debt, fixed charges, exemptions, supplemental assessments and policy responses. A negative gap means reported tax exceeds this benchmark.

04. What this cannot establish

Tax records and estimates can refer to different years. The annual comparison is a cross-date descriptive measure, not a same-year effective tax rate. Estimates can be inaccurate, especially for unusual properties.

Observed gaps cannot be attributed entirely to Proposition 13. No causal model, full market reappraisal or revenue simulation is performed. Missing values are excluded from the relevant comparison and remain blank in the CSV.

Owners’ names, contact details and raw browser captures are excluded. The operational crawl dashboard remains local and is not part of this public site.
